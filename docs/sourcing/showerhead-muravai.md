## Read this first: the claim doesn't hold in the Netherlands

- **Dutch tap water has no chlorine**: "The Netherlands is one of the few countries where chlorine is not used at all", neither to disinfect the water nor to keep a residual in the pipes ([Smeets et al. 2009](https://library.kwrwater.nl/publication/51462918/the-dutch-secret-how-to-provide-safe-drinking-water-without-chlorine-in-the-netherlands/)). On 22 Oct 2026 the Consumentenbond called filter ads that promise to remove chlorine, lead and mercury misleading: "chloor wordt niet eens gebruikt in Nederland" ([ANP/Hart van Nederland](https://www.hartvannederland.nl/advies-en-tips/waarschuwen/artikelen/consumentenbond-misleidende-waterfilterreclames)).
- **Germany** chlorinates only where it is needed, at most 0.3 mg/L free chlorine after treatment ([UBA list, Bundesanzeiger 27 Jan 2023](https://www.bundesanzeiger.de/pub/publication/IH2o2fSsV5YvYknFqru/content/IH2o2fSsV5YvYknFqru/BAnz%20AT%2027.01.2023%20B12.pdf)).
- **The US** uses chlorine or chloramine everywhere, and more than one American in five gets chloramine ([EPA](https://19january2025snapshot.epa.gov/dwreginfo/chloramines-drinking-water)), which a shower cartridge barely touches: carbon needs about 10 minutes of contact to break it down, and a shower gives about a second ([WQA 2014](https://wqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2014_Chloramine.pdf)).
- **The only shower-filter standard sets a low bar.** NSF/ANSI 177 tests free chlorine only, and a filter passes with a 50% reduction. It doesn't test heavy metals or chloramine ([WC&P](https://wcponline.com/2004/12/15/new-standard-shower-filters-2/), [P&M](https://www.pmmag.com/articles/87589-web-exclusive-br-nsf-ansi-standard-177-a-level-playing-field-for-shower-filter-claims)). Muravai is not in NSF's listing of 7 certified companies ([NSF, 1 Nov 2026](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/DWTU/Listings.asp?Standard=177)).

**What this means for Connor:** in NL and DE, sell the pressure, the massage head, the stop button and a filter that catches sediment and rust. Don't promise chlorine, lead or "99.9%". In the US the filter claim is real, but it needs proof (a lab report on your exact cartridge). And it won't do much against chloramine.

## What matters in a supplier for this product

What buyers complain about, from 120 one- and two-star Trustpilot reviews of Muravai, Hello Klean, Jolie and Stone Stream: no noticeable effect (22), weak pressure (14), subscriptions and billing (13), slow shipping (13), **cracked plastic (11)**, **leaks (10)**, refunds (9), a filter that seizes or clogs (9), rust (4), a cartridge or thread that doesn't fit (3). These are counts from a small sample, not rates. On Amazon, the aspect counts of a top seller point the same way: PWERAN's "Durability" has 458 negative mentions out of 572, and "Leakage" 214 out of 300 ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CGRFJC9M)). No credible public defect or return rate exists for this kind of showerhead (**unknown**).

What separates a good unit from a bad one:

1. **Real filter media, and enough of it.** Documented KDF shower filters hold 368–510 g of KDF ([Multipure manual](https://Www.Multipure.com/content/mp-aquashower-manual.pdf), [KDF/Sprite test](https://kdf.kymerainternational.com/research/lab_sprite)). KDF-55 is gold-coloured copper-zinc granules, about 2.7 g/cm³ ([KDF bulletin](https://www.wecofilters.com/media/productattach//k/d/kdf55_85sheet.pdf)), and it doesn't stick to a magnet. Activated carbon is black and about 0.5 g/cm³ ([datasheet](https://store.newterra.com/products/gc-12x40s-coconut-shell-based-granular-activated-carbon-gac)). Calcium sulfite comes as hard beads ([US 6,056,875](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6056875A/en)). "Stages" are layers: Muravai names 4 materials for its 18 stages. A brown filter shows rust or sediment caught in the cotton, or vitamin C breaking down. It isn't a measure of chlorine used up ([Pentair](http://www.pentair.com/en-us/home-water-treatment/filter-replacements/rs24-cartridge.html), [Multipure](https://Www.Multipure.com/content/mp-aquashower-manual.pdf)).
2. **No cracks.** The cracks buyers report are at the thread root, the connector and the filter housing: where moulding stress meets a tightened nut. Ask for virgin ABS and its grade. Test with drops and an over-tightened nut.
3. **No leaks.** The button, the mode ring, the O-ring of the filter housing and the hose nuts. Each joint needs a flat washer, and the housing's O-ring should sit in a groove.
4. **Flow, not "pressure".** A head can't raise the water pressure. Small holes make the jet faster, and a restrictor keeps the flow legal. California treats a hand shower as a showerhead: 1.8 gpm (6.81 L/min) at 80 psi at most ([CA Plumbing Code 408.3](https://gocodebook.com/library/us/ca/ca-plumbing-code/chapter-4-plumbing-fixtures-and-fixture-fittings/408.3-water-consumption.-showerheads-shall-have-a)). A Dutch shower averages 7.7 L/min for 7.4 minutes: 57 L a shower, 46 L per person per day ([CBS, WGT 2021, table 6.2.2](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/longread/aanvullende-statistische-diensten/2022/watergebruik-thuis--wgt---2021/6-de-douche)).
5. **The hose.** A good EU shower hose is double-interlocked stainless steel with brass nuts and a non-twist swivel, made to EN 1113 ([Delabie example](https://www.delabie.com/export/productsheet/pdf/49525?inline=1)). A magnet says nothing about "304" stainless in a worked hose strip ([Atlas Steels](https://atlassteels.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Atlas-Tech-Note-No-11-Magnetic-Response-of-Stainless-Steel-17-06-21.pdf)). Ask for the material certificate.
6. **Threads.** EU hand showers connect with a G1/2 parallel thread sealed by a washer. US shower arms are 1/2-14 NPT, tapered and sealed with tape ([BSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Standard_Pipe), [NPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_pipe_thread)). In NL/DE the hose goes onto the mixer, so the arm mount and teflon tape are mostly useless there. Offer an EU set without them, and ship US sets with the mount.
7. **Brass and lead.** CW602N brass, often sold as the "safe" alloy, contains 1.7–2.8% lead ([datasheet](https://www.smithmetal.com/pdf/brass/cz132.pdf)). C69300 ("eco brass") has 0.02–0.09% ([Concast](https://www.concast.com/c69300.php)). For the US, "lead free" means 0.25% or less, weighted across the wetted surfaces ([EPA](https://epa.gov/sdwa/questions-and-answers-about-final-lead-free-rule)). Ask for the alloy of every brass part.
8. **The cartridge.** It must open by hand after weeks of use (Jolie and Hello Klean buyers report housings that seize), and it must fit every batch. Without a steady refill supply there's no subscription.
9. **The stop button.** In the UK, stop-button heads on electric instantaneous showers have scalded people. They are fine on mixer showers ([Warwickshire Trading Standards](https://www.warwickshire.gov.uk/news/article/4962/warwickshire-trading-standards-act-after-resident-is-burnt-by-dangerous-showerhead)). Put a "not for electric showers" warning in the box.

## Sample tests

The scorecard holds these 11 tests; the three marked **must pass** put a supplier out on one fail. Order 3 units and 3 spare cartridges from each of the top 3, and keep one Muravai unit as the bar. Equipment: a 10 L bucket, a stopwatch, a 0.1 g kitchen scale, paper towels, a magnet, a saw, a thermometer; for the US chlorine test a DPD kit such as the Hach CN-66 ([Hach](https://www.hach.com/p-chlorine-free-total-color-disc-test-kit-model-cn-66/223101)). The protocol follows the shower-filter structural tests of NSF/ANSI 177 (watertight after hot water and cycling) where they apply; the thresholds are suggestions.

| Test | Pass |
|---|---|
| **Leak** (must pass): 15 min of hot water at full pressure with the stop button closed, then each mode, a dry paper towel round every joint | Every towel dry |
| **Filter teardown** (must pass): cut one cartridge open, weigh each layer, work out g/cm³ | KDF-55 present (gold, about 2.7 g/cm³, not magnetic); the grams within 20% of the supplier's list; not mostly filler balls |
| **Drop** (must pass): 5 times from 1 m onto tiles, then tighten the nut 1/8 turn past firm | No crack, no leak in a re-run of the leak test |
| Flow: litres in 60 s at your pressure, each mode | EU: 6 L/min or more at about 3 bar. US: 6.8 L/min (1.8 gpm) or less |
| Spray force: head 45 cm above a kitchen scale with a plate | 57 g (2.0 oz) or more, as WaterSense asks at 20 psi ([spec](https://epa.gov/sites/default/files/2017-01/documents/ws-products-spec-showerheads.pdf)) |
| Fit | EU: on a G1/2 hose with flat washers, no tape. US: on a 1/2-14 NPT arm with the mount |
| Hose: hang 20 kg for 60 s, twist the head 10 turns | No leak, no kink; the swivel turns |
| Cartridge: open and close 20 times; use daily for a week | Opens by hand; flow down less than 20% |
| Chlorine, US water only: free chlorine before and after, at 40 °C, read within a minute | 80% less or better on a new cartridge (the NSF floor is 50% over its life) |
| Box and labels | Undamaged; no other brand; room for your GPSR details; the electric-shower warning |
| Delivery timed | Order to doorstep days match the quote |

In NL the chlorine test reads zero before and after: the tap water has none. Test it in the US, at a lab, or with water dosed to about 2 mg/L. For the first stock order, have the batch inspected before it ships at AQL 0 critical / 2.5 major / 4.0 minor ([HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/aql-sampling-in-quality-control-inspections/)), and count any leak or crack as major.

## The refill cartridge, and what $19.99 every 28 days leaves

**What Muravai sends:** a generic PP-cotton "stick", a white fibre tube with a clear cap and two pegs. Its own data gives 25 g for a 3-pack: about 8 g a cartridge ([products.json](https://muravai.com/products.json), [3-pack](https://muravai.com/products/3-pack-filters)). That is the weight of a plain PP-cotton stick (5–15 g on the Amazon listings below). A cartridge with real KDF-55 or calcium sulfite in it would weigh noticeably more. This is an inference from the weights, so weigh and cut one open before you repeat its "18-stage" claim.

**Fix in writing before the first order:**

1. **A drawing of the cartridge**, with tolerances: its length with the caps, its diameter, and the caps, pegs and O-ring.
2. **The handle mould's number and revision.** An approved cartridge stays approved only for the mould it was tested in.
3. **The grams of each filter material.**
4. **A slim box** that fits a letterbox (at most 38 × 26.5 × 3.2 cm, at least 14 × 9 cm), with a batch code and an EAN.
5. **A sealed reference sample** that both sides keep.
6. **No change** to the mould, the media or the packaging without your written approval.

To keep the supply going for 12+ months:

- Hold 3–6 months of refills at the warehouse: demand is one shipment per subscriber every 28 days.
- Qualify a second factory with the same reference sample before you need it.
- Put ownership of any mould in a written tooling agreement, with non-disclosure, non-use and non-circumvention terms ([Harris Sliwoski](https://harris-sliwoski.com/zh/chinalawblog/who-owns-your-molds-how-overseas-factories-hold-tooling-hostage-and-how-to-stop-them/), snippet).
- Agree a 12-month forecast with 3 months of firm orders. The published lead times are 20–35 days.

**Ship refills from EU stock, never from China.** A refill sent from China pays €3 + €2 + VAT ≈ **€5.42** before postage. A bulk batch pays 1.7% duty (TARIC 8421 21 00) and lands at about **€0.59 / €2.50 / €5.06 a 3-pack** (low / base / high: plain PP sticks by sea, carbon and calcium sulfite by sea, Greenwell's sticks by air). A 3-pack box up to 3.2 cm thick goes as a PostNL letterbox parcel: about €3.74–4.55 in NL, and from €3.75 to Germany. These rates are from search snippets, because postnl.nl was down when checked ([MyParcel 2026 specs](https://www.myparcel.nl/app/uploads/myparcel-vervoerdersspecificaties-2026-nl.pdf), [PostNL](https://www.postnl.nl/zakelijke-tarieven/partijenpost)). Add the 3PL's €2.50–3.50 to pick and pack it.

**Use a flat-fee subscription app** while volumes are small: Kaching $9.99–24.99 a month, Appstle $0–100 and Seal $0–24.95, all with no fee per order. Recharge is $149 a month plus 1.5% + 20¢ per order ([Kaching](https://apps.shopify.com/kaching-subscriptions), [Appstle](https://apps.shopify.com/subscriptions-by-appstle), [Seal](https://apps.shopify.com/seal-subscriptions), [Recharge](https://apps.shopify.com/subscription-payments)). **iDEAL can't pay the renewals**: Shopify says local, non-card methods may not work for subscriptions ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/subscriptions/considerations)). Dutch subscribers will pay by card, PayPal or Apple Pay.

**What one €19.99 refill leaves** (a 3-pack, about 200 refills a month, before ads, returns and overheads):

contribution = price ÷ (1 + VAT) − payment fee (1.9% + €0.25) − app fee − landed refill − packaging − pick and pack − postage

Pick and pack plus postage is 38–71% of the net price; the cartridge is the small part. A refill loses money when the box is too thick for a letterbox, when it goes as a full parcel to Germany, or when a per-order app fee meets low volume. **Sell one cartridge every 28 days in a letterbox box**, not Muravai's 3-pack: it is honest to the "change every 4 weeks" rule and it leaves more. The packaging cost is an assumption (€0.20–0.90), and the US margin is not worked out here. If €19.99 a month for one cartridge meets resistance (the brands above charge $6–13 a month), 3 cartridges every 12 weeks at €18.95 leaves about €5 a parcel in the NL base case, with a third of the parcels to send.

**Over six months** (scenarios, not data): at 12.5% of subscribers stopping before each refill, which matches the 45% that Recharge says are still subscribed after six months ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/blog/announcing-recharges-interactive-2023-state-of-subscription-commerce-report/)), a subscriber takes about 3.9 refills: **€77 gross, about €20 contribution** in the NL base case. At 5% it is €101 and €26, and at 30% €41 and €11. Category churn for shower filters is unknown. Recharge's newer numbers, from 65.1 million Shopify subscriptions started March 2025 to February 2026, are harder: "About two-thirds of subscribers continue past their first order, and under 40% are still around by the third" ([Recharge, Aug 2026](https://getrecharge.com/blog/the-quarterly-upgrade-subscription-retentions-most-underused-lever/), vendor data). That's nearer the 30% case: plan on €11–20 per subscriber.

**The law for a refill subscription in NL and DE:**

- The 14-day right to withdraw runs from the **first** delivery ([§356 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__356.html), [art. 6:230o BW](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0005289/2026-07-16)).
- A **withdrawal button** has been required since 19 Jun 2026 ([§356a BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__356a.html), [art. 6:230oa BW](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0005289/2026-07-16)).
- Germany also requires a **"Verträge hier kündigen"** button. Without it, the customer can cancel at any time without notice ([§312k BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312k.html)).
- A refill that keeps renewing must be cancellable at any time with at most a month's notice ([§309 Nr. 9 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__309.html), [art. 6:236 j BW](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0005289/2026-07-16), [ACM](https://www.acm.nl/nl/onderwerpen/verkoop-aan-consumenten/verkopen-aan-consumenten/abonnementen-en-leveringscontracten-aanbieden)).
- Next to the order button, show the price, the cadence and "cancel any time". The button must say *zahlungspflichtig bestellen* / *bestelling met betalingsverplichting* or something as clear ([§312j BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312j.html), [art. 6:230v BW](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0005289/2026-07-16)).
- The subscription is an opt-in the buyer ticks, never a default: see Muravai's cart rule above, and art. 22 of the directive.
- Not required for goods in NL or DE (we found no such rule), but worth it: an e-mail 3–7 days before each refill ships, with skip and cancel links. It heads off chargebacks. Hello Klean sends one 3 days before.
- **The US, later:** the terms before the card details, express consent, and a simple way to stop ([ROSCA](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403)). California since 1 Jul 2025: express consent, cancel online "at will", a reminder every year ([Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/california-tightens-requirements-for-automatically-renewing-subscriptions)). A court threw out the FTC's "click to cancel" rule in July 2025; the FTC started again in March 2026 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule)).
- **Watch:** the EU's Digital Fairness Act, due in mid-November 2026, is aimed at subscription traps ([European Parliament briefing](https://eprs.europarl.europa.eu/contents/publications/EPRS/2026/10/EPRS_BRI%282026%29791529.html)).

So the offer to make is "€19.99 every 28 days, no minimum term, cancel any time".

## The rules: EU and US

**In short:** the EU side is mostly papers a good factory can give you: a REACH report, your GPSR file and labels, and **no CE mark**. The US side is heavier: a DOE certification, flow marking, a California listing, and 23–41% duty on every parcel. That is one more reason to start in NL and DE. Researched on 3 Nov 2026; this is research, not legal advice, and the tariff code needs a binding ruling before the first bulk import.

### EU (NL and DE)

| Topic | What applies |
|---|---|
| CE mark | **None, and it must not be printed.** No CE directive covers a non-electric showerhead, and the head and hose standards EN 1112 and EN 1113 are not harmonised ([NLF list of CPR standards](https://www.nlfnorm.cz/en/cpr/skupina/11)). A CE mark may only go where EU law provides for one ([Reg. 765/2008](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2008/765/oj), Art. 30). A factory that prints one on this product doesn't know the rules. | Product safety (GPSR) | **Mandatory.** Under your own brand you are the manufacturer: a risk assessment and a technical file, a type or batch number on the product, your name and address and an EU contact, safety text in Dutch and German, the same details on every listing, and accident reports through the Safety Business Gateway ([Reg. | Countdown timers, "only today", "almost sold out" | **No**, unless it's true (the blacklist, Annex I point 7 of the directive). An EU sweep found more than half of such cases misleading ([Commission, 26 Mar 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_734/IP_26_734_EN.pdf)) | The same (UWG, Anhang Nr. 7) | A real end date, or nothing |
| "Buy 1, get 1 free" | Fine, if the price of one isn't inflated to pay for the free one ([Commission guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2018-05/ucp-guidance-en.pdf)) | The same (UWG, Anhang Nr. 20) | — |
| "Money back guarantee" | Only if you take used units back: a shower-filter shop's "100% geld terug garantie" was misleading when it refused them ([RCC 2020/00276](https://www.reclamecode.nl/uitspraken/resultaten/huishouden-en-inrichting-2020-00276/278669/)) | The same (§ 5 UWG) | The 14-day withdrawal right, and your own terms in plain words |

**Who acts on it:** in the Netherlands, ACM can fine up to €900,000 a breach ([ACM](https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/acm-waarschuwt-voor-nepkortingen)), and consumers and competitors complain to the Reclame Code Commissie. In Germany, competitors and the Wettbewerbszentrale send a cease-and-desist letter (Abmahnung), backed by a contractual penalty: that's the usual route, and it's fast. Meta rejects health claims as well (the board's Risk radar flags them).

### US

| Topic | What applies |
|---|---|
| Flow | 2.5 gpm at 80 psi at most. **California** (and Washington) 1.8 gpm, and the model must be listed in California's appliance database (MAEDbS) **before** it is sold there ([20 CCR §1608](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/20-CCR-1608)). "California legal flow rate" is only safe with that listing. WaterSense (2.0 gpm) is voluntary. |
| DOE certification | **The importer counts as the manufacturer:** certify each model with the DOE before selling, then every year by 1 March ([10 CFR 429.12](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/chapter-II/subchapter-D/part-429/subpart-B/section-429.12), [429.29](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/chapter-II/subchapter-D/part-429/subpart-B/section-429.29)). |
| FTC marking | The flow rate in gpm and L/min on the product and the box, and a "water use" disclosure next to the price online ([16 CFR 305.24](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-305/subpart-C/section-305.24), [305.27](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-305/subpart-C/section-305.27)). |
| Lead | Whether the federal "lead free" rule covers showerheads is **unclear** ([EPA](https://epa.gov/sdwa/questions-and-answers-about-final-lead-free-rule)): use lead-free brass anyway. California's Prop 65 needs a warning when lead, DEHP or BPA exposure goes above its safe-harbour levels: online, on the product page or through a link there that says "WARNING" ([27 CCR 25602](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/27-CCR-25602)). |
| Backflow, claims | Hand showers need backflow protection (IPC 424.2). The FTC wants proof for "3rd-party tested" and "removes 99.9%", and NSF/ANSI 177 covers free chlorine only. For health claims it wants "competent and reliable scientific evidence" ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)). An "up to" figure has to be what buyers normally get ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2012/06/ftc-report-many-consumers-believe-claims-promise-maximum-results)). A class action filed on 19 Mar 2026 says Jolie's "remove chlorine, heavy metals & other contaminants" head cut chlorine by only about 66% ([ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/news/class-action-lawsuit-claims-jolie-filtered-showerheads-leave-substantial-amount-of-chlorine-in-water); claims, not findings). |
| "Engineered in the USA" | The FTC fined a water-filter seller $110,000 for "designed and crafted in USA" on filters made in China ([FTC, 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/04/marketer-water-filtration-systems-pay-110000-civil-penalty-deceptive-made-usa-advertisements)). Leave it out. |
| Reviews | The FTC's rule against fake and suppressed reviews has applied since 21 Oct 2024, with civil penalties ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). Showing another product's reviews cost a seller $600,000 ([FTC, Bountiful](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/02/ftc-charges-supplement-marketer-hijacking-ratings-reviews-amazoncom-using-them-deceive-consumers)). Use your own reviews only. |
| Duty | **No de minimis**: every parcel pays ([CBP rule, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-24/html/2026-12670.htm)). On Chinese goods, about 23–41% since 24 Jul 2026, depending on the code: the base rate, the older Section 301 lists, and a new 12.5% Section 301 duty ([USTR, 23 Jul 2026](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/FLIP%20301%20Investigation%20Final%20Action%20FRN%207-23-26%20FINAL.pdf)). |
