# Choosing a supplier, every time

The method behind the board's **🏭 Suppliers** tab (🚀 Launch → Suppliers), for every product. It puts **product quality and fast delivery first** and price after, as Connor asked. The filtered showerhead is the first product run through it: [showerhead-muravai.md](showerhead-muravai.md).

## How do I choose every time?

Shortlist 5 to 8 listings and send the same questions to the best 5 (the message is below, in English and Chinese). Put each answer into the scorecard as it comes in: one supplier per column, up to 5. Drop any supplier that fails a hard gate: no samples, no certificates the product needs, delivery slower than your limit, an identity you can't check, or no way to get a defect replaced or refunded. Buy one unit from the competitor as your quality bar. Order samples from the top 3 and test them all the same way, timing the delivery from order to doorstep. Then choose the top-scoring supplier that fails no gate, write down why, and name the next one as your backup. The board saves the decision with its price, delivery days and lead time. Check it again before every reorder and every 90 days: the price, new complaints and the delivery days. If the choice fails a gate, or another supplier now scores 5+ points higher, switch to the backup.

## The steps

| # | Step | What goes into the scorecard | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shortlist** 5 to 8 listings: image search, sorted by sales. Prefer factories with 2+ years on the platform and a high repeat-buyer rate. | A supplier per column: name, link, route, where it ships from | Day 0 |
| 2 | **Send the first message** to the top 5 (copy it from the scorecard, in English or Chinese, and attach a photo of the product) | Unit cost, shipping, days, lead time, MOQ; the gates they answered; Reliability (how fast and how clearly they answer) | Day 0 to 2 |
| 3 | **Buy one unit from the competitor**: the quality bar for the samples | | Day 0 |
| 4 | **Order samples** from the top 3: 2 or 3 units each, shipped the way customers' orders would be | Order date | Day 1 to 2 |

| 6 | **Test the samples** with the product's tests, next to the competitor's unit, and time the delivery | Each test, pass or fail; the measured days (tick "measured"); Quality follows | Day 5 to 12 |

| 8 | **Re-check** before every reorder and every 90 days | A re-check line: price now, complaints, days | Every reorder; 90 days |

The board's checklist (Setup and record → Checklist) follows these steps, so a scorecard shows how far you are.

## Hard gates

One fail and the supplier is out, whatever its score: it goes to the bottom of the ranking and the board won't let you choose it. **Open** means not known yet. You can choose a supplier with open gates for samples and test orders, but confirm them before you pay for stock: the board says which ones.

| Gate | Passes | Fails | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Samples** | You can order 1 to 3 units before buying stock | No samples, or only with a stock order | Ask; order them |
| **Certificates** | It has the test reports this product needs for your market (the product's setup lists them) | None, refuses to share them, or they belong to another company | Check the report number with the lab that issued it. The applicant must be this supplier, or the factory behind it. A product that needs none: tick "needs no certificates" and the gate passes |
| **Delivery days** | Order to doorstep, in your market, within your limit | Slower than your limit | The board checks it from the days you fill in. **Your limit is a setting** (default 12 days). Use the measured days once you have them |
| **Identity** | A business licence, a verified company on the platform, or a real factory you have seen on video | It can't or won't show who it is | Business licence; the platform's verification; a video call or a dated video of the line |
| **Defects** | A replacement or refund for defective units, in writing or under the platform's buyer protection | No way to get anything back | Ask for it in writing; check that the platform's protection covers the order |
| **Sample tests** | Every must-pass test passed | A must-pass test failed (for a showerhead: it leaks, or the filter is fake) | The product's tests, marked "must pass" (one per line, starting with !). No must-pass test set: nothing to fail |

## The score

Seven criteria, scored 0 to 5, weighted. **Quality and delivery count most** (25 each). The weights are a setting per product (Setup and record → Market, limits and weights).

| Criterion | Weight | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 | Auto |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product quality evidence | 25 | Sample passed every test | Sample passed the must-pass tests | No sample yet: real photos, the inside, repeat buyers | Listing only: photos and sales | Weak: stock photos, claims that do not add up | Failed a test, or fake parts | From the sample tests, once a sample is tested |
| Delivery time to the customer | 25 | 3 days or less | 4 or 5 days | 6 or 7 days | 8 to 10 days | Within your limit | Over your limit | From the days to your market |
| Reliability and communication | 15 | Answers within a day, clear, 3+ years, verified | Answers within a day, clear | Answers within 2 days, some vague | Slow or vague | Evasive, or contradicts itself | No answer | You score it |
| Landed cost and margin | 15 | Break-even ROAS 1.6 or lower | Up to 2 | Up to 2.5 | Up to 3 | Above 3 | Loses money on every order | From your price and its landed cost |
| Compliance and certificates | 10 | Reports for your market, checked at the lab | Reports for your market, not checked yet | Reports, not for your market or not theirs | Can get reports (time, cost) | No reports, vague | Refuses, or fake reports | You score it |
| Returns and defect handling | 5 | In writing: free replacement or refund | In writing, with limits | Platform protection only | Case by case | Replacement at your cost | No way to get anything back | You score it |
| Scalability, MOQ and branding | 5 | Low MOQ, logo and box, 10,000+ a month | Logo or box at a fair MOQ | No branding yet, capacity fine | High MOQ or small capacity | Cannot grow with you | Cannot supply after the first batch | You score it |

**Score** = 100 × Σ (score ÷ 5 × weight) ÷ Σ weights. A criterion that isn't scored counts as 0, so missing evidence never helps a supplier. The table shows "auto" next to a score the board worked out, and your own score always wins over Auto.

**How Auto works:**

- **Delivery:** the days from order to doorstep in your market (for "EU and US", the slower of the two): 3 or less → 5, 4 to 5 → 4, 6 to 7 → 3, 8 to 10 → 2, more but within your limit → 1, over it → 0 (and the gate fails).
- **Cost, with your price filled in:** the break-even ROAS of one order, worked out the same way as the launch card's margin: your price minus VAT, payment fees and the landed cost. Landed cost = units per order × unit cost + shipping, plus the EU duties and fees when the order reaches an EU customer from outside the EU and the price isn't DDP.
- **Cost, without your price:** each supplier against the cheapest one still in the running (5 × cheapest ÷ its cost, at least 1).
- **Quality:** from the sample tests: 1 + 4 × tests passed ÷ all tests. A test not done counts as not passed, so a half-tested sample can't get a 5. A failed must-pass test gives 0.

**Ties** go to the better quality score, then the faster delivery, then fewer gates still open, then the lower landed cost, then the supplier added first.

**What a score means:** 75 or more with every gate passed: choose it. 60 to 75: fine for samples and test orders, get the missing evidence before stock. Under 60: you don't know enough yet. The board flags it.

## The sample protocol (every product)

1. **Order 2 or 3 units from each of the top 3**, shipped the way a customer's order would be: the same line, to your own address in NL (or a friend's in DE or the US). Note the order date.
2. **Have the competitor's unit next to them.** Every sample has to be at least as good.
3. **Run the product's tests** on every unit (the scorecard lists them; the showerhead's are in [its analysis](showerhead-muravai.md#sample-tests)). Use the same method and order for each supplier. Photograph each result.
4. **Mark each test pass or fail** in the scorecard, per supplier. A must-pass fail puts that supplier out.
5. **Time the delivery**: order date to doorstep, in days. Put the measured days in the supplier's days and tick "measured".
6. **Check the box and labels**: undamaged, no other brand on it, and room for what EU law needs on the product (your name and EU address, a type or batch number, warnings).

Tests that fit almost any product (the defaults for a new scorecard): works as described (every function, 10 times); a 1 m drop onto tiles three times with no crack or rattle; matches the listing (size, colour, material, weight); packaging and labels; delivery timed.

## The first message

The scorecard writes it from the product's questions and numbers them, in English and in Chinese, ready to copy. Send it with a photo of the product. These are the questions a new scorecard starts with. Add the product's own (what's inside, what it's made of, the certificates it needs) under **Questions for suppliers**, one per line, as `English | 中文`.

**English**

> Hello,
> I run an online store in the Netherlands and Germany (and the US), and I am comparing suppliers for this product (see the photo). Could you answer these questions?
> 1. Are you the factory or a trading company? Please send your business licence and a short video of the production line today.
> 2. Price per unit at 50, 200 and 1,000 units?
> 3. Lead time from payment to dispatch for a sample, 200 units and 1,000 units?
> 4. Packed weight and size of one unit, units per carton, carton size and weight?
> 5. Can you ship single orders to customers in the Netherlands and Germany (and the US)? Which line, how many days door to door, and are the duties and fees included (DDP/IOSS)?
> 6. Test reports for the EU (CE, RoHS or REACH, as the product needs): report number, lab, and the applicant's name?
> 7. What is your defect rate, and do you replace or refund a defective unit for free? In writing, please.
> 8. Your logo and packaging: MOQ and cost?
> 9. Is the sample the same as bulk production, and is the sample fee refunded on a bulk order?
> 10. Monthly capacity, and can you keep stock for me?
> Thank you!

## Red flags

Each of these means: ask again, test harder, or drop the supplier.

- **The certificate doesn't check out**: the lab can't find the report number, the applicant is another company, or the report covers another product. It's a gate fail.
- **No samples, or the sample isn't the bulk product** ("the sample is special"). Ask in writing: sample = bulk.
- **A price far below the others** for what looks like the same product. The difference is usually in what you can't see (for the showerhead: the filter media).
- **Stock photos only**, no real photo or video of the product, its inside or the factory. Or photos that also appear under other sellers' names.
- **Vague or changing answers**: a lead time that moves, "yes" to everything, no numbers.
- **Pressure to pay outside the platform**, or to a personal account. Pay through the platform or your agent, so the buyer protection applies.
- **Many recent complaints about the same fault** in its reviews (leaks, cracks, "not as described").
- **Delivery quoted in "working days" from dispatch, not from the order**: add the handling time and weekends before you compare.
- **No written defect policy.**
- **Claims you can't use**: "removes 99.9% of heavy metals", "cures dandruff", "NSF certified" without a listing you can find. If you copy them into your ads without proof, the risk is yours. What an ad may say in NL and DE, claim by claim: [the showerhead's rules](showerhead-muravai.md#what-an-ad-may-say-in-nl-and-de).
- **A borrowed certificate**: a certificate for a material or a part, shown as if it covered the whole product. For example, "KDF certified to NSF/ANSI 42" covers the raw filter media, not the cartridge ([Kymera](https://kymerainternational.com/product/kdf-55/)). Look the exact model up in the certifier's own listing on the day you check: listings change ([NSF/ANSI 177 listing](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/DWTU/Listings.asp?Standard=177)).
- **Certificate icons on Alibaba cards**: the supplier declares them, and Alibaba doesn't check them against a certificate. Ask for the report itself.
- **"Stages" or "layers" that are mostly filler**, or the same ingredient counted twice. One retail "15-stage" shower filter lists vitamin C twice, next to "magnetic energy" and "far infrared" balls ([B&Q listing](https://DIY.com/departments/spares2go-universal-shower-head-15-stage-filter-hard-water-purifier-softener-chrome-effect-/5057817439344_BQ.prd)).

**The pattern that fits quality and speed first:** test with samples and an agent's DDP line, then move the winner into EU stock as soon as it sells. For the batch from the factory, ask for an inspection before shipment at AQL 0 critical / 2.5 major / 4.0 minor, the usual levels for consumer goods ([HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/aql-sampling-in-quality-control-inspections/)). Count any leak or crack as major, and anything that can scald or shock as critical.

## The decision record

**Choose** on a supplier in the ranking keeps a record that stays with the product:

- **Who:** the supplier's name and link, its route, and where it ships from.
- **The numbers then:** unit cost, shipping to NL/DE and to the US, landed cost per order, delivery days to the customer, lead time for stock, MOQ.
- **The score:** the total, each of the 7 parts, the weights used, and the gates still open on that day.
- **The backup:** the next-best supplier that fails no gate, or one you pick.
- **Why:** your note.
- **When:** the date, and the next re-check (90 days on).

A new decision replaces the old one, and **Take the decision back** undoes it. Both keep the old record in the history (the last 10). The board won't remove a supplier while it is your choice or its backup.

On a product with a launch card, **Choose** also makes it the supplier chosen in 🔎 Sourcing. Its link, unit cost, shipping, MOQ and lead time go into the launch card's Sourcing step, and its margin follows. The record says whether that worked: a supplier without a unit cost, or priced in £, can't be chosen there.

## Re-checking

**Before every reorder, and every 90 days**, look at the chosen supplier again:

1. **Price:** the unit cost and the shipping now. Change them in its column if they moved.
2. **Quality:** new complaints from your customers and in its reviews; the defect rate of your last batch.
3. **Delivery:** the days your last orders took from order to doorstep.
4. **Papers:** test reports still valid; any new rules for the product or for parcels.

Tap **Re-checked today** with a note. The board keeps the line and sets the next re-check 90 days on. Once the date passes, the scorecard shows a red "Re-check overdue" flag.

**Check right away** when your customers' complaints about the same fault climb, when delivery goes over your limit, when the price goes up 10% or more, or when the supplier runs out of stock. When the choice now fails a gate, or another supplier scores 5+ points higher, the board flags it: switch to the backup.

## In the board

**🚀 Launch → 🏭 Suppliers.** The tab lists every scorecard. Start one from a launch card (**🏭 Score them side by side** in its 🔎 Sourcing panel, **🏭 Compare suppliers** in its Sourcing step, or **Start** in the list), or for any product by name and link.

On a launch card's product the scorecard works with 🔎 Sourcing:

- The links pasted there show up here, one tap each.
- **Choose** here chooses the same supplier there, so the 💶 € view and the launch card's margin use it.
- After that, 🔎 works with its own numbers for that supplier: edit a price there, and the € view follows.
- 🔎 converts ¥ at its "¥ for €1" rate. It defaults to 8.3, but the ECB rate was 7.46 on 2 Nov 2026, so set today's rate in 🔎 or ¥ costs come out about 10% low.
- The launch card counts the EU fees as €3 + €2 per order, without the VAT on the €2. Its margin can differ from the scorecard's by €0.42 an order.

A scorecard has:

- **On top:** the verdict (*Choose X*, *Best now: X*, *Your choice: X*), the backup, the flags (red first), and the ranking with a **Choose** button on each supplier that fails no gate.
- **Side by side:** a column per supplier, best first. It scrolls sideways on a phone.
- **Suppliers:** up to 5. Each one holds its facts (unit cost, shipping, days, lead time, MOQ, years on the platform, repeat buyers, hours to answer), its gates, its sample tests and its scores.
- **Setup and record:** the checklist; the sample tests; the questions, with the message to copy; the market, the delivery limit, your price, units per order, VAT, payment fees, the EU duties per order, the certificates the product needs and the weights; and the decision record with its re-checks.

Everything saves as you type, in the D1 table `sourcing` (migration `0060_sourcing.sql`). The scoring code is `worker/src/supplier.mjs` (`supplierKit()`). The board runs the same code while you type (`/supplier.js`), and `test/supplier.test.mjs` tests it.
